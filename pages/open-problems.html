Open problems

These questions remain open to my knowledge. Last status check: 16 November 2026.

Graphs and directed cuts

Problem1Oriented cycle double cover

Does every finite bridgeless multigraph admit a multiset of circuits, each given a cyclic orientation, such that every edge is traversed exactly once in each direction? Parallel edges may form a 2-cycle, but traversing one edge forward and immediately back is not a circuit.

The unrestricted existence question is discussed in Gáborik et al., Manhattan and Chebyshev flows, p. 9.

Problem2Global edge-bicut

Given a finite digraph G=(V,E) with at least two vertices and nonnegative rational arc costs encoded in binary, find a minimum-cost set of arcs whose deletion leaves two vertices s,t such that neither can reach the other by a directed path. The vertices s,t are chosen as part of the solution. Is this problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Bérczi et al. distinguish this global edge-deletion problem from the fixed-terminal and vertex-deletion variants and leave its complexity open in A tight 2​-approximation for linear 3-cut, p. 413.

Problem3Global linear 3-cut

Given a finite digraph G=(V,E) with at least three vertices, find a minimum-cardinality arc set F such that G−F has at least three strongly connected components. Is this solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

This is the global problem, with no prescribed terminals. See my thesis, Definition 1.2 and Table 1.2.

Problem4Fast rooted connectivity certificates

Let G=(V,E) be a finite digraph with n vertices, m arcs, and a root r. For x=r, let λG​(r,x) be the maximum number of arc-disjoint directed paths from r to x. For an integer k≥1, can one construct a subgraph H=(V,F) with F⊆E and ∣F∣=O(kn) satisfying λH​(r,x)≥min{λG​(r,x),k} for every x=r in total time O(n+km), or even O(n+m)?

The existence of such sparse subgraphs follows from Szeszlér’s connectivity-preserving subgraph theorem, Theorem 8. The question concerns construction time. The O(n) term accounts for vertices that may be unreachable from the root.

Problem5Deterministic hedge min-cut

Fix an integer k≥2. Let G=(V,E) be a finite connected undirected multigraph with at least two vertices, and let a partition of E into nonempty classes, called hedges, be given. For each hedge, the subgraph formed by its edges and their incident vertices has at most k connected components. Is there a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm finding a minimum number of hedges whose removal disconnects G?

Chandrasekaran et al. give a randomized polynomial-time algorithm when the number of components per hedge is bounded, as a special case of Hypergraph k-Cut in Randomized Polynomial Time, Theorem 1.1. The question asks for derandomization.

Cut counting

Problem6Hypergraph minimum k-cut-sets

Fix an integer k≥3. Let H=(V,E) be a finite hypergraph with n≥k vertices and positive hyperedge costs. For a partition of V into at least k nonempty parts, its cut-set consists of the hyperedges meeting more than one part, and its cost is the sum of their costs. Is the number of distinct minimum-cost cut-sets O(nk) for every fixed k, with no bound on hyperedge size?

The known upper bound is O(n2k−2), while examples give Ω(nk) distinct minimum cut-sets. Beideman et al. ask for a tighter bound in Deterministic enumeration of all minimum cut-sets and k-cut-sets in hypergraphs for fixed k, §7. The objects counted here are hyperedge sets, so different vertex partitions with the same cut-set count once.

Submodular optimization

Problem7Minimum separated submodular 3-partition

Let f:2U→R be submodular on a finite set U with ∣U∣≥3, given by a value oracle, and let s,t∈U be distinct. Can one find nonempty pairwise disjoint sets A,D,C covering U, with s∈A and t∈D, minimizing f(A)+f(D)+f(C) using polynomially many oracle calls and arithmetic and comparison operations in ∣U∣, independently of the magnitudes of the oracle values?

Bérczi et al. study separating submodular partitions in {s,t}-Separating Principal Partition Sequence of Submodular Functions, §1.1.1. Their approximation guarantees and results for a variable number of parts leave this exact three-part question separate.

Problem8Ranked enumeration of submodular 3-partitions

Let f:2U→R be submodular on a finite set U with ∣U∣≥3, given by a value oracle. Give an unordered partition P of U into three nonempty parts the value Φf​(P)=∑P∈P​f(P). Can every such partition be output exactly once in nondecreasing order of value, with ties in any order, so that for some polynomial p and every r up to the total number of outputs, producing the first r partitions takes at most (r+1)p(∣U∣) oracle calls and arithmetic and comparison operations? Termination must be certified within another p(∣U∣) operations after the final output.

A minimum three-partition can be found in polynomial time. Ranked enumeration for two parts is recorded in Hirayama et al., A polynomial time algorithm for finding a minimum 4-partition of a submodular function, Lemma 5.2. The question asks for ranked enumeration of three-partitions.

Constraint satisfaction

Problem9Dichotomy for surjective minimum violation

Fix a finite digraph H=(D,F) with D=∅, allowing loops. Given a finite digraph G=(V,E) with ∣V∣≥∣D∣ and binary-encoded nonnegative integer arc weights w, minimize ∑uv∈E:(ϕ(u),ϕ(v))∈/F​w(uv) over surjections ϕ:V↠D. Characterize the patterns H for which this problem is polynomial-time solvable. Does every fixed pattern give either a polynomial-time solvable problem or an NP-hard problem?

Kawarabayashi and Xu formulate the classification question in Minimum violation vertex maps and their applications to cut problems, §1 and §7. Their results include a surjective dichotomy for reflexive trees, while the question here allows arbitrary finite digraphs.

Matroids and delta-matroids

Problem10Gabow’s serial symmetric exchange conjecture

Let M be a finite matroid of rank r with disjoint bases B1​,B2​. Are there orderings B1​={x1​,…,xr​} and B2​={y1​,…,yr​} such that, writing Xi​={x1​,…,xi​} and Yi​={y1​,…,yi​} with X0​=Y0​=∅, both (B1​∖Xi​)∪Yi​ and (B2​∖Yi​)∪Xi​ are bases for every i∈{0,…,r}?

Akrami et al. state the general conjecture in Matroids are Equitable, Conjecture 1.14. It is known for several restricted classes, including split matroids and regular matroids.

Problem11Finite-forbidden-set proximity for even delta-matroids

Let E be finite and let D⊆2E be nonempty. Write X△Y for the symmetric difference of sets X,Y. Suppose the members of D all have the same cardinality parity and satisfy symmetric exchange: for X,Y∈D and e∈X△Y, some f∈(X△Y)∖{e} satisfies X△{e,f}∈D. Such a pair (E,D) is an even delta-matroid.

Let Γ be an abelian group, choose γ0​,γe​∈Γ and c0​,ce​∈R for e∈E, and define λ(X)=γ0​+∑e∈X​γe​ and c(X)=c0​+∑e∈X​ce​. Let F⊆Γ be finite, and suppose some X∈D satisfies λ(X)∈/F. Must every minimizer A of c over D admit a minimizer B of c over {X∈D:λ(X)∈/F} satisfying 21​∣A△B∣≤∣F∣?

The corresponding proximity theorem for matroid bases appears in Liu et al., Proximity Theorem and FPT Algorithm for Minimum Group-labeled Matroid Bases, Theorem 1. The question here concerns arbitrary even delta-matroids.

Matching

Problem12Deterministic Exact Matching

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected graph, let R⊆E be a designated set of red edges, and let r be an integer. A perfect matching is a set of edges incident to each vertex exactly once. Is there a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm deciding whether G has a perfect matching M with ∣M∩R∣=r?

A randomized polynomial-time algorithm is known. Sebő discusses the deterministic question for general graphs in Matchings: Source, Goal and Faithful Companion, p. 6.

Problem13Minimum-cost Exact Matching

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected graph, let R⊆E be a designated set of red edges, and let r be an integer. Each edge e has a nonnegative integer cost c(e) encoded in binary. A perfect matching is a set of edges incident to each vertex exactly once. Is there an algorithm running in time polynomial in the total input bit length that finds a perfect matching M of minimum cost ∑e∈M​c(e) subject to ∣M∩R∣=r, or reports that none exists? Randomized algorithms succeeding with probability at least 2/3 on every input are allowed.

Armbruster et al. describe the binary-cost optimization question as open even for bipartite graphs in Multiplicative Assignment with Upgrades, §§5.2–5.3. Known randomized pseudopolynomial algorithms can depend on the numerical costs rather than their bit lengths.

Allocation

Problem14Labelled-bin filling

There are n≥1 bins and m≥1 item types. The input consists of positive integer weights w1​<⋯<wm​ with w1​∣⋯∣wm​, nonnegative integer preferred counts aij​, supplies Bj​, and targets hi​, all encoded in binary. Find nonnegative integer counts xij​ minimizing ∑i=1n​∑j=1m​∣xij​−aij​∣ subject to j=1∑m​wj​xij​i=1∑n​xij​​=hi​≤Bj​​​(1≤i≤n),(1≤j≤m),​ or report that no feasible counts exist. Let N be the total input bit length.

Is the problem polynomial-time solvable for every fixed n≥3, including n=3 with wj​=2j−1? More strongly, is there an algorithm running in g(n)NO(1) time for some computable function g, or is the decision problem with a binary-encoded cost threshold W[1]-hard parameterized by n? With n and m unrestricted, is there an exact algorithm running in poly(N,wm​) time?

The underlying optimization model is stated in my question on filling bins with minimum moves. The bound poly(N,wm​) allows dependence on the largest weight’s numerical value while keeping the dependence on counts, supplies, and targets polynomial in their bit lengths.

Discrete geometry

Problem15Covering a square by six unit squares

Can six closed squares of side length 1 in the Euclidean plane cover an entire square of side length strictly greater than 2? The unit squares may be translated and rotated independently, and overlaps are allowed.

Four unit squares tile a square of side length 2. Dósa et al. conjecture that six cannot cover a larger square in Covering a square by congruent squares, Conjecture 1.1. Their Theorem 1.3 proves that five cannot, while seven suffice.
